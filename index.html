Method

Assumptions

Cell cost = materials + scrap loss + labour + energy + capital recovery + overhead, per kWh of good cells. Capital is recovered at 8% over 10 years. Scrap inflates every conversion cost by 1 / (1 − scrap); 20% of scrapped material value is recovered.

The benchmarks reproduce published figures within 2%: China NMC811 $57/kWh and Germany $99/kWh (BNEF BattMan via T&E), EU 17% above the US, and LFP 90% above China. Material costs, US parameters, wages and pack adders are my own assumptions, set to match those anchors.

What this model leaves out

  • Material prices are held at China benchmark levels in every region, plus a small logistics premium. Real sourcing contracts, tariffs and lithium price swings would move the materials line.
  • Subsidies, production tax credits and EU tariffs on Chinese cells are excluded. They change who captures the gap, not its size.
  • Material carbon footprints are held equal across locations. Sourcing lower-carbon cathode material in Europe would widen Sweden's advantage.
  • Plant-level data on scrap, uptime and ramp curves would sharpen Exhibits 1 and 3 most. That is the data a battery team with client access holds.

  2. BloombergNEF (Dec 2025), Battery price survey — $108/kWh global pack average, $84 in China.
  3. IEA (2026), Global EV Outlook 2026 — cathode 40–50% of NMC and 25–30% of LFP cell cost; EU ~15% of 1.2 TWh EV battery demand; ~3 TWh global by 2030; yields above 90% needed.
  4. Roland Berger (2025), Two outlooks for battery manufacturing in Europe — equipment 50–100% and buildings 200–400% more expensive in Europe.
  5. Chico (2026), Europe's battery cost gap starts at the ramp-up — citing Fraunhofer FFB and RWTH PEM: 15–30% scrap in early years, ~10% after five.
  6. Ember 2025 grid intensities (via GreenCalculus) — China 0.526, Germany 0.330, Poland 0.589, Sweden 0.035, US 0.350 kg CO₂/kWh.
  7. T&E (2023), Battery carbon footprint position paper — NMC811 footprints of 64–105 kg CO₂e/kWh by production location.
